# Scale of Health-Insurance Claim Rejections, Deductions, Delays and Disputes in India (Patient + Hospital Side), 2023–2026

Research date: 3 Oct 2026. "FY25" = April 2024–March 2025. All ₹ figures are in crore unless stated (1 crore = 10 million; 1 lakh = 100,000).
Primary-source note: the IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25 PDF was downloaded and the text pulled out directly. Figures marked **[IRDAI AR 2024-25, primary]** were read from its tables (Tables I.26–I.29, II.15, II.17, Annexure 11). Other figures come from secondary press coverage and are labelled that way.

---

## Q1. What do IRDAI annual reports say about health claims (FY24, FY25)?

### Takeaway
In FY25, insurers deducted ("disallowed") **₹18,521 crore** from health claims and repudiated another **₹11,412 crore (29.5 lakh claims)**. Together that is about **₹29,900 crore** not paid, up about 15% from about ₹26,000 crore in FY24. That is roughly ₹30 of every ₹100 actually paid out (₹94,248 crore). Deductions grew faster than repudiations, which supports a deductions/partial-settlement angle for ClaimDesk.

### Cited Findings

**FY2024-25 (IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25, Table I.29 "Status of Claims under Health Insurance", general + standalone health insurers, excl. PA/travel)**
- Claims outstanding at the start of FY25: 20.73 lakh claims / ₹7,584.58 crore. New claims registered in FY25: 351.85 lakh / ₹1,24,903.22 crore. Total on the books: **372.58 lakh claims / ₹1,32,487.81 crore** — [IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25 (primary, p.43)](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- Claims paid: **326.01 lakh claims / ₹94,247.60 crore** (87.50% by number, **71.14% by amount**) — [IRDAI AR 2024-25](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- **Claims disallowed "as per terms and conditions of policy contract": ₹18,521.02 crore = 13.98% of claim amount** (the table reports no number of claims for disallowances, because these are partial deductions on claims that were otherwise paid) — [IRDAI AR 2024-25](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- **Claims repudiated: 29.51 lakh claims (7.92%) / ₹11,412.42 crore (8.61%)** — [IRDAI AR 2024-25](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- **Claims outstanding at end of FY25 (31 Mar 2025): 17.07 lakh claims (4.58%) / ₹8,306.76 crore (6.27%)** — [IRDAI AR 2024-25](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- Report text: insurers "settled about 87 per cent of total number of claims registered… repudiated about 8 per cent… remaining about 5 per cent were pending for settlement as on 31st March 2025" — [IRDAI AR 2024-25, para I.6.5.6](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- Average amount paid per claim in FY25: **₹28,910** — [IRDAI AR 2024-25, para I.6.5.5](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- **Cashless vs reimbursement, FY25 (Table I.28, by number / by amount):** Only cashless 188.95 lakh claims (57.96%) / ₹62,537 crore (**66.35%**). Only reimbursement 119.58 lakh (36.68%) / ₹27,649 crore (29.34%). Both modes 4.33 lakh (1.33%) / ₹2,912 crore (3.09%). Benefit-based 13.14 lakh (4.03%) / ₹1,150 crore (1.22%) — [IRDAI AR 2024-25](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- **TPA vs in-house, FY25:** 69% of claims (224.08 lakh; ₹55,091 crore) were settled through TPAs and 31% (101.93 lakh; ₹39,157 crore) in-house. Within TPA-handled claims, 60.84% by number were cashless — [IRDAI AR 2024-25](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- **Incurred Claims Ratio (ICR), FY25 vs FY24 (Table I.27):** Total 86.98% (FY24: 88.15%). Public sector insurers 100.59% (103.38%). Private general insurers 87.59% (88.71%). **Standalone health insurers 68.73% (64.71%)**. For individual business specifically, SAHIs ran at 69.08%, compared with 95.04% for PSUs — [IRDAI AR 2024-25](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- Net incurred health claims FY25: ₹84,850 crore, up about 11% — [IRDAI AR 2024-25, para I.6.5.4](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- Definitions used by IRDAI: "rejected" = cannot be considered under policy terms and conditions; "repudiated" = cannot be considered under Section 45 of the Insurance Act, 1938 — [IRDAI AR 2024-25 (life claims table note)](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- IRDAI publishes only aggregate repudiation rates, not the grounds for denial by insurer. A March 2026 parliamentary reply (Lok Sabha Unstarred Q. 5816, answered 30 Mar 2026) confirmed that IRDAI does not collect reasons for individual denials — [Insurance Business Asia, 27 Jul 2026](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/life-insurance/irdai-cannot-explain-why-health-insurance-claims-go-unpaid-583814.aspx); [Taxguru](https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/health-insurance-claim-rejections-valid-based-policy-conditions-government.html)

**FY2023-24 (IRDAI Annual Report 2023-24, as reported by press)**
- Total health claims on the books: about ₹1.17 lakh crore. Paid ₹83,493.17 crore (71.29%). **Disallowed ₹15,100 crore (12.9%)**. **Repudiated ₹10,937.18 crore (9.34%)**. Outstanding ₹7,584.57 crore (6.48%) — [Outlook Business, 30 Dec 2024](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/health-insurers-disallowed-claims-worth-rs-15100-crore-during-fy24); [Business Standard, 30 Dec 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/health-insurers-disallowed-claims-worth-rs-15-100-cr-during-fy24-irdai-124123000562_1.html)
- About 3.26 crore claims, of which 2.69 crore were settled (82.46%). Average paid per claim ₹31,086. 72% settled via TPAs and 28% in-house. 66.16% of claims by number were cashless and 39% reimbursement — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/health-insurers-disallowed-claims-worth-rs-15100-crore-during-fy24)
- Disallowed plus repudiated FY24 ≈ **₹26,000 crore, up 19.10% YoY** — [Business Standard, 27 Dec 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/health-insurance-claims-rejection-up-19-10-in-fy24-irdai-report-124122700754_1.html); [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/insurance/story/health-insurance-claim-rejections-by-insurance-companies-went-up-by-1910-in-fy24-458593-2024-12-26)
- Secondary press put FY25 rejected/repudiated plus disallowed at about **₹30,000 crore (+15% YoY)** — [Business Upturn, 30 Apr 2026 (updated 14 Jul 2026)](https://www.businessupturn.com/sectors/health/indias-health-insurance-claim-rejection-crisis-rs-30000-crore-denied-in-one-year-and-irdai-is-finally-cracking-down). This matches the primary data: ₹18,521 + ₹11,412 = ₹29,933 crore.

**Average settlement time:** neither the FY24 nor the FY25 IRDAI annual report gives an industry-wide average health-claim turnaround time (see Gaps). The regulatory standards are: decide cashless pre-auth within **1 hour**, final discharge authorisation within **3 hours** (effective 1 Aug 2024), with any extra hospital charges from a delay paid from the insurer's shareholder funds. Reimbursement claims must be settled within 30 days, with interest at 2% above bank rate on delay (IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance, 29 May 2024) — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/health-insurance-decide-cashless-request-in-1-hour-provide-final-authorisation-for-discharge-within-3-hours-says-irdai/74269.html); [The Week, 30 May 2024](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2024/05/30/irdai-issues-new-master-circular-to-make-health-insurance-claims-process-more-seamless.amp.html)
- June 2025: IRDAI inspections flagged gaps in following the health master circular at 8 insurers (New India, ICICI Lombard, HDFC ERGO, Tata AIG, Star Health, Niva Bupa, Care Health, ManipalCigna). Issues included claims-settlement handling and the composition of the Claims Review Committee — [CAalley/Business Standard, 26 Jun 2025](https://www.caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/irdai-flags-issues-at-8-insurers-over-health-master-circular-norms)

### Inferences
- **The headline ClaimDesk stat (FY25):** ₹29,933 crore of health claims was deducted or repudiated. That is about **₹82 crore a day**, or **22.6% of the ₹1.32 lakh crore claimed** (13.98% + 8.61%).
- **Deductions are the bigger and faster-growing problem.** Disallowances rose from ₹15,100 crore to ₹18,521 crore (**+22.7% YoY**). Repudiations rose only from ₹10,937 crore to ₹11,412 crore (+4.3%). For every ₹100 paid, insurers deducted about ₹19.65 (18,521 ÷ 94,248). Deductions mostly hit cashless claims, so the hospital desk (and the patient at discharge) feels them most.
- About 1 in 12 claims (29.5 lakh) was repudiated outright in FY25, and 17 lakh claims were still pending at year-end.
- SAHIs paid out about 69 paise per premium rupee, compared with about ₹1.01 for PSU insurers. That gap is often cited by critics as evidence of stricter claims management at standalone health insurers. This is an inference, not an IRDAI finding.
- Cashless share by number appears to fall from 66.16% (FY24, press) to 57.96% (FY25, primary). This may be a definitional change, because FY25 splits out "both" and "benefit-based" claims. Do not present it as a trend without checking the FY24 primary table.

### Gaps
- No industry average claim turnaround time (TAT) is published in the IRDAI annual reports. IRDAI's insurer-wise "claims TAT" disclosures sit in public-disclosure forms (NL-forms) of individual insurers, which were not compiled here.
- The FY24 figures above come from press coverage of the FY24 annual report, not a direct read of the FY24 PDF. The "3.26 crore claims" figure appears for both FY24 (registered) and FY25 (settled), so some articles may have mixed them up.
- The FY2025-26 IRDAI annual report (normally released Dec–Jan) is not yet out as of Oct 2026. A OneAssure page titled "IRDAI Annual Report 2026" exists but was not verified.
- The ₹ value of claims "rejected" (policy terms) versus "repudiated" (Section 45) is not split for health. IRDAI reports a single repudiated line.

## Q2. Insurance Ombudsman / CIO: health complaints, share, grounds, outcomes

### Takeaway
About 3 in 5 complaints that reach the Insurance Ombudsman are health complaints (37,431 in FY25). Where the Ombudsman disposes of a health complaint, 41% end with the policyholder winning or a mediated settlement. That suggests many denials do not hold up when challenged.

### Cited Findings
- **FY25 health complaints received by Ombudsman: 37,431 (FY24: 34,203)**. Disposed: 29,017 (FY24: 29,276) — [Cafemutual, 2 Jan 2026, citing CIO Annual Report 2024-25](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/36635-41-of-health-insurance-complaints-were-resolved-in-favour-of-policyholders-in-fy25)
- FY25 health outcomes: in favour of complainant 6,126 (16.37%). Recommendation/mediated settlement 9,417 (25.16%). **Combined 41% favourable (FY24: 55%)**. In favour of insurer 4,897 (FY24: 4,122). Withdrawn 3,649. Non-entertainable 4,928 — [Cafemutual](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/36635-41-of-health-insurance-complaints-were-resolved-in-favour-of-policyholders-in-fy25)
- FY25 all-lines total: 60,304 complaints handled by 18 Ombudsman centres, 48,898 disposed. Health is described as about 64% of complaints, and about 69% of complaints relate to claims — [Insurance Business Asia](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/life-insurance/latest-india-data-finds-complaints-clustered-in-insurance-segment-565197.aspx)
- FY24 all-lines (CIO Annual Report 2023-24, primary): 17 centres received **52,575** complaints and disposed of 49,705 (12,855 non-entertainable). 15,528 were resolved via recommendation/mediation (more than 42% of entertainable disposals). About 87% were disposed within 90 days — [CIO Annual Report 2023-24 (primary)](https://cioins.co.in/annualreports/AnnualReport2023-2024.pdf). On that base, health = 34,203 / 52,575 ≈ **65%** of FY24 Ombudsman complaints.
- Top insurers by FY25 Ombudsman complaints: **Star Health 12,186**, Care Health 4,423, Niva Bupa 3,983, National Insurance 1,890 — [Insurance Business Asia](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/life-insurance/latest-india-data-finds-complaints-clustered-in-insurance-segment-565197.aspx); see also [Business Standard, 1 Sep 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/star-health-care-niva-bupa-record-most-policyholder-complaints-in-fy24-125090100487_1.html) (headline says "FY24". **Year label is disputed** between sources.)
- Most Ombudsman grievances fall under Rule 13(1)(b) of the Ombudsman Rules, 2017 (partial or total repudiation of claims), with Star Health accounting for more than 10,000 such cases. Star Health had **52.30 complaints per 10,000 claims in FY25, against an industry average of 27.06** — aggregator summary of search results [(Oquilia / Algates; not verified against primary)](https://www.oquilia.com/news/star-health-vs-niva-bupa-claims-fy25)
- Regional example: of 4,314 complaints at the Ahmedabad Ombudsman in FY25, 3,540 (**82.06%**) were health-insurance complaints — [Gujarat Samachar](https://english.gujaratsamachar.com/news/national/gujarat-insurance-complaints-4314-cases-reach-ahmedabad-ombudsman-in-2024-25-53368328074)
- Ombudsman compensation ceiling was raised from ₹30 lakh to ₹50 lakh. Insurers must comply with awards within 30 days or pay a penalty of ₹5,000 per day — [IRDAI AR 2024-25 (primary)](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)

### Inferences
- In FY25, about 15,500 disposed health complaints ended in the policyholder's favour or in a mediated settlement. That is evidence that a meaningful share of denials and deductions are reversible when properly contested. This supports the "contest" half of ClaimDesk.
- The fall in favourable outcomes (55% to 41%) could mean better insurer decisions or tougher Ombudsman scrutiny. The data does not tell us which.

### Gaps
- The CIO Annual Report 2024-25 PDF is a scanned image (no text layer), so the ground-wise split of health complaints (total repudiation vs partial repudiation vs delay) could not be pulled out. The FY24 report's tables are also images. The ground-wise breakdown and the per-10,000-claims figures are **not verified from the primary source**.
- There is a mismatch on FY25 health's share of Ombudsman complaints: 37,431 / 60,304 = 62% versus "about 64%" in press.

## Q3. IRDAI grievance data (Bima Bharosa / IGMS)

### Takeaway
Grievances against general and health insurers rose about 45% in one year (FY24 to FY25). In FY26, health insurance alone generated about 1.18 lakh grievances on Bima Bharosa, nearly as many as all of life insurance.

### Cited Findings
- **FY25 Bima Bharosa:** 2,57,790 total grievances, of which 1,20,429 were life and **1,37,361 general & health** (FY24: 94,843, so **+44.8%**). Public-sector general/health insurers had **8,201 grievances pending** at end-FY25 (FY24: 4,055) — [IRDAI AR 2024-25, Tables II.15 and II.17 (primary)](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- In FY25, **69% of general-insurance grievances were claims-related**. There were 88,841 claims-related grievances in general and health, against 11.26 crore claims — [IRDAI AR 2024-25, paras II.6.8–II.6.9](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- Across all lines, claims-related complaints were 49.04% in FY25 (1,26,412 of 2,57,790), up from 46.85% in FY24 and 41.45% in FY23. Top grounds in FY25: non-disposal of claims 15.07%, claim repudiation without reasons 6.54%. Insurers with the most grievances in FY25: LIC 74,104, **Star Health 20,527**, National 12,859, **Care Health 10,281**, United India 10,126 — [Cafemutual, 18 Mar 2026](https://cafemutual.com/news/insurance/37260-number-of-claims-related-complaints-in-insurance-industry-rose-to-49-in-fy25)
- **FY26 Bima Bharosa (Lok Sabha reply to Shashi Tharoor):** 2,97,468 grievances received and 2,87,496 disposed. **Health: 1,17,979 received, 1,12,315 disposed, 5,664 pending.** Life: 1,19,027. Non-life (excl. health): 60,462 — [Cafemutual, 10 Aug 2026](https://cafemutual.com/news/insurance/38460-insurance-industry-disposed-off-287-lakh-complaints-in-fy26-through-bima-bharosa)

### Inferences
- About 1.18 lakh health grievances in FY26 works out to roughly 320 per day.
- FY25 and FY26 are not directly comparable, because FY25 combines general and health while FY26 separates them.

### Gaps
- No public category breakdown of health-only grievances (cashless delay vs deduction vs rejection) for FY26 was found.

## Q4. Consumer surveys (LocalCircles and others), 2023–2026

### Takeaway
Repeated large LocalCircles surveys find that only about a third of claimants report full, smooth approval. Partial approval is now the most common bad outcome. Half or more of cashless patients wait more than 6 hours for discharge approval, despite IRDAI's 3-hour rule.

### Cited Findings
- **LocalCircles, released 7 Mar 2026** (54,000 responses, 323 districts; 18,989 answered the claims question). Outcomes: **15% rejected "with invalid reasons"**, **29% partially approved "with invalid reasons"**, 15% partially approved with valid reasons, 32% fully approved, 9% approved after back-and-forth. **Discharge** (18,119 respondents): **50% waited 6–48 hours**, 22% waited 3–6 hours, 8% were instant (the IRDAI norm is 3 hours). **Premiums**: 21% saw increases of more than 200% over 3 years (2022–25) — [LocalCircles press release](https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/health-insurance-claim-survey); [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/health-insurance-premiums-rise-sharply-while-claim-experiences-remain-difficult-finds-localcircles-survey/79872.html)
- **LocalCircles, ~Jan 2025** (30,366 policyholders). Of those who claimed in the past 3 years: 25% fully approved, 6% fully approved after back-and-forth, **33% partially approved with invalid reasons**, **36% rejected with invalid reasons**, i.e. **more than 50% faced rejection or partial approval**. Discharge: **6 in 10 waited 6–48 hours** (21% waited 24–48 hours). Only 8% were instant — [BusinessToday, 2 Jan 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/insurance/story/insurance-claims-over-50-health-cover-claims-faced-rejection-or-partial-approval-says-survey-459394-2025-01-02); [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/6-in-10-health-insurance-claimants-waited-648-hours-for-claim-approval-and-discharge-from-hospital-localcircles/76013.html)
- **Insurance Samadhan (claims-resolution firm), Mar 2025:** analysed 9,928 grievances. **88% of complaints were about claim rejection.** Millennials (25–38) filed 46% of them. Top reasons: non-compliance with policy terms, sum insured exhausted, misrepresentation/non-disclosure, and hospitalisation not medically necessary or below the minimum duration — [BusinessToday, 12 Mar 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/insurance/story/88-of-consumer-complaints-are-due-to-insurance-claims-being-rejected-report-467731-2025-03-12)
- An Insurance Samadhan report (Sept 2025) said insurance complaints were up 45% in Q2 2025, mostly about health policies — [Business Standard, 24 Sep 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/insurance-complaints-up-45-in-q2-2025-most-about-health-policies-report-125092401017_1.html) (headline only; full text not accessed)
- A 2024 study cited by HDFC ERGO and Health Insurance Sahi Hai reported that **43% of policyholders faced difficulties** with claims, some waiting an extra day in hospital, and that nearly half faced rejections over 3 years — [Health Insurance Sahi Hai](https://healthinsurancesahihai.com/health-insurance-claims-in-india-43-policyholders-faced-difficulties-some-had-to-wait-an-extra-day-at-hospital-survey/); [HDFC ERGO news](https://www.hdfcergo.com/news/health-insurance/study-says-nearly-half-of-indian-policyholders-faced-health-insurance-claim-rejections-in-the-last-three-years) (underlying survey not verified)

### Inferences
- The surveys are self-selected (LocalCircles members) and capture perceived invalidity. They show much worse outcomes than IRDAI's 8% repudiation rate. The two can be reconciled because surveys count partial approvals (deductions) as failures, while IRDAI counts them as "paid".
- Between the Jan 2025 and Mar 2026 surveys, outright rejection fell (36% to 15%) but partial approval with invalid reasons stayed high (33% to 29%). That is consistent with IRDAI's data showing deductions growing faster than repudiations.

### Gaps
- The Policybazaar claims report (BW Healthcare) was blocked (403) and not verified. No Beshak claim-rejection dataset was found.

## Q5. Hospital-side pain (AHPI, insurers, deductions, receivables)

### Takeaway
2025 was a year of open conflict between hospitals and insurers. AHPI (15,000+ member hospitals) threatened cashless suspensions against Bajaj Allianz, Care Health and Star Health. Max Healthcare was removed from or suspended by Niva Bupa, Star, Care and Tata AIG networks. The complaints were "unilateral deductions", delayed payments, slow pre-auth and discharge approvals, and tariffs frozen at 2017–2022 levels. No hard industry-wide figure on hospital denial or deduction rates or receivable days was found from a credible source.

### Cited Findings
- **Bajaj Allianz (Aug 2025):** AHPI advised its member hospitals (15,000+, including Max and Medanta per press) to suspend cashless for Bajaj Allianz policyholders from 1 Sep 2025. AHPI cited "unilateral deductions by the company, delay in payments and unduly high time taken for issuing pre-auth and pre-discharge approvals" plus outdated tariffs, and sought revisions in line with 7–8% medical inflation (Dr Girdhar Gyani, DG, AHPI). The suspension was withdrawn on 29 Aug 2025 after talks — [Business Standard, 22 Aug 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/insurance/15k-hospitals-suspend-cashless-services-bajaj-allianz-policyholders-125082201318_1.html); [BusinessToday, 25 Aug 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/insurance/story/healthcare-providers-body-calls-for-suspension-of-cashless-treatment-for-bajaj-allianz-policyholders-490905-2025-08-25); [The Week, 29 Aug 2025](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/08/29/dcm36-biz-ahpi-bajaj-allianz.html)
- **Care Health (Aug 2025):** AHPI notice dated 2 Aug 2025 sought a response by 31 Aug. The advisory was later withdrawn together with Bajaj's — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/ahpi-revokes-cashless-suspension-for-bajaj-allianz-and-care-health-policyholders)
- **Star Health (Sep 2025):** AHPI threatened suspension from 22 Sep 2025, alleging "unfair deductions from bills even after approval", rejection of claims after final approval, and de-empanelment without notice. The threat was withdrawn, services were reinstated from 10 Oct 2025, and parties agreed to resolve tariffs by 31 Oct 2025. Hospitals said tariffs were fixed in 2017/2019. Insurers countered with treatment costs rising 15–20% a year at top hospitals, price variation (appendectomy ₹30–40k at some hospitals vs over ₹1 lakh at others), and allegations of inflated billing — [Moneylife, 26 Sep 2025](https://moneylife.in/article/hospitals-vs-insurers-tariff-battles-put-patients-in-the-crossfire/78365.html)
- The General Insurance Council publicly pushed back, saying the suspensions would "prejudice the interests of policyholders" — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/personal-finance/cashless-care-at-risk-gi-council-pushes-back-what-policyholders-should-know-if-services-stop)
- **Max Healthcare network exits (Aug–Sep 2025):** Niva Bupa removed all Max hospitals from its network from 16 Aug 2025 (agreement expired May 2025). Max said Niva Bupa sought further cuts on rates already "pegged at 2022 levels". Tata AIG suspended cashless at Max from 10 Sep 2025 despite a signed tariff agreement running from 16 Jan 2025 to 15 Jan 2027. Star Health and Care Health also suspended cashless at Max — [Business Standard, 2 Sep 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/cashless-no-more-niva-bupa-customers-to-pay-at-max-hospitals-claim-later-125090200136_1.html); [IANS, 26 Sep 2025](https://ianslive.in/tata-aig-suspends-cashless-services-at-max-hospitals-across-india--20250926141327); [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/news-brief/tata-aig-joins-star-health-niva-bupa-in-ending-cashless-claim-settlement-at-max-hospitals)
- **Government schemes:** AHPI said CGHS/ECHS bills worth at least ₹1,000 crore were pending. A list of 61 CGHS-empanelled private hospitals owed more than ₹500 crore, including Max (over ₹192 crore), Fortis (over ₹67 crore), Apollo (over ₹62 crore) and Narayana (over ₹29 crore) — search-result summary of [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/govt-owes-rs-500-cr-to-hospitals-under-cghs-376466) (**date not confirmed; may be pre-2023**)
- **Hospital revenue from insured patients:** Max Healthcare hospital revenue mix is 37.8% insurance and corporates, 32.5% self-pay, 20.9% institutional, 8.8% international. Fortis and Max each earn about ₹2,000 crore from insurance patients. Listed-hospital insurance revenue grew from ₹2,800 crore to ₹7,700 crore over FY21–FY24 (35% CAGR) — [Way2Wealth Healthcare thematic, 9 Apr 2025](https://www.way2wealth.com/reports/RR09042025ece95.pdf) (broker research; secondary)
- Hospital revenue-cycle vendor claims (low reliability, not primary): TPA claims make up 20–40% of billing revenue in many Indian hospitals, and "rejection rates of 10–20% of submitted claim value" are routine — [AccredReady blog](https://accredready.in/learn/tpa-insurance-claim-denials) (**vendor marketing content; unverified; do not quote as fact**)
- Medical associations say negotiated insurer rates are 30–50% below regular rates, and hospitals have complained of receiving TPA dues after 6–8 months — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/insurance-company-admits-to-delayed-payments-to-hospitals/7379.html) (**older article, likely pre-2020; context only**)
- **Network scale:** at 31 Mar 2025, the 17 active TPAs held **2,73,816 hospital network agreements** (+55,342 added, −12,333 removed in FY25). Hospitals can tie up with more than one TPA, so this is not a count of unique hospitals — [IRDAI AR 2024-25, Annexure 11 (primary)](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- System responses: the GI Council has started work on common empanelment of hospitals on one platform, rate negotiations, and integration of all hospitals into the NHA's National Health Claims Exchange (NHCX) — [IRDAI AR 2024-25, para II.9.2(v)](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf). BIS is developing a common billing template and QCI is grading hospitals for tariff alignment — [Moneylife, Sep 2025](https://moneylife.in/article/hospitals-vs-insurers-tariff-battles-put-patients-in-the-crossfire/78365.html). As of mid-2024, 34 insurers/TPAs were live on NHCX — [Digital Health News](https://www.digitalhealthnews.com/33-major-health-insurance-companies-boarded-nha-s-nhcx-platform)

### Inferences
- Hospitals take the hit from the FY25 ₹18,521 crore disallowance pool. When an insurer deducts from a cashless bill, the hospital either collects the balance from the patient at discharge (friction, bad debt, reputation) or writes it off. The split is not public.
- The 2025 disputes show that tariffs and deductions, not outright rejections, are the main hospital grievance. A tool that justifies line items against tariff, policy terms and clinical notes before submission speaks directly to the deductions complaint AHPI kept raising.

### Gaps
- **No credible, quantified industry figure** was found for: hospital denial or deduction rate (% of billed value), receivable days on insurance/TPA dues, or % of revenue lost to deductions. Listed-hospital DSO (days sales outstanding) by payor was not found in the sources reviewed. AHPI/FICCI/NATHEALTH have not published survey numbers that turned up in searches.
- Whether the AHPI–Star 31 Oct 2025 tariff deadline produced an agreement, and whether 2026 saw new suspensions, was not confirmed.

## Q6. Notable cases 2024–2026 on unfair rejections

### Takeaway
Consumer forums keep ruling against insurers on non-disclosure and pre-existing-disease (PED) denials. Several cases turn on documentation errors, which is exactly the problem a hospital-desk assistant could catch before submission.

### Cited Findings
- **NCDRC, 3 Oct 2025, Niva Bupa:** the insurer repudiated a 78-year-old's claim for alleged non-disclosure of PED, based on a **hospital case-sheet error recording symptoms as "two and a half years" instead of "two and half weeks"**, and cancelled the policy. NCDRC dismissed Niva Bupa's revision petition and upheld orders to pay ₹9,20,827 with 6% interest from Aug 2016, plus ₹32,000 in loyalty benefits and ₹50,000 compensation. It found deficiency in service and unfair trade practice — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/niva-bupa-health-insurance-ordered-to-pay-rs92-lakh-mediclaim-rs82000-compensation-to-78yearold-policyholder/78639.html)
- **NCDRC (June 2024):** an insurer cannot reject a claim for non-disclosure if it issued the policy after assessing the insured's health, even if some proposal columns were blank — [Business Standard, 11 Jun 2024](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/insurers-cannot-deny-claims-for-non-disclosure-after-issuing-policy-124061100760_1.html); related Care Health case at [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/consumer-cases/ncdrc-insurer-repudiate-insurance-non-disclosure-care-health-insurance-deficiency-in-service-259987)
- **NCDRC:** a mediclaim cannot be denied on a presumption of PED — [Medical Dialogues](https://business.medicaldialogues.in/mediclaim-cannot-be-denied-on-presumption-of-pre-existing-disease-ncdrc)
- **Delhi State Commission (2026):** a claim cannot be rejected without proof of PED; the insurer was directed to pay ₹20 lakh (a life-insurance case) — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/consumer-cases/insurance-claim-cannot-be-rejected-without-proof-of-pre-existing-disease-delhi-state-commission-directs-insurer-to-pay-20-lakh-532782)
- Other insurer-pays orders: Star Health ordered to pay ₹15.30 lakh in hospital expenses and costs — [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/star-health-and-allied-insurance-asked-to-pay-rs1530-lakh-towards-hospital-expenses-costs/72261.html)
- **Room-rent proportionate deduction:** when a patient takes a room above the policy's room-rent sub-limit, insurers scale down room-linked charges (room, nursing, OT, doctor fees) proportionately, though not medicines, implants or diagnostics. This is a recurring source of partial settlements — [Business Standard, 14 Nov 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/partial-hospital-bill-payout-boost-policy-cover-to-secure-full-claims-125111401478_1.html)
- **Moratorium:** under the May 2024 master circular, the moratorium period (after which a claim cannot be contested except for proven fraud) was cut from 8 to 5 years — **not verified from the circular in this session**; widely reported in coverage of the [May 2024 master circular](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2024/05/30/irdai-issues-new-master-circular-to-make-health-insurance-claims-process-more-seamless.amp.html).

### Gaps
- No systematic count of consumer-forum health-insurance cases was found (the NCDRC/e-Jagriti dashboards were not queried).
- Reported "IRDAI March 2026" measures (100% of genuine claims settled within 15 days of the final discharge summary, automatic approval with compound-interest penalties, hospital network suspension within 15 days for cashless misuse, a forthcoming hospital–insurer code of conduct) appear only in [Business Upturn](https://www.businessupturn.com/sectors/health/indias-health-insurance-claim-rejection-crisis-rs-30000-crore-denied-in-one-year-and-irdai-is-finally-cracking-down), with no circular number. **Unverified; do not quote without checking irdai.gov.in.**

## Q7. Market size and context

### Takeaway
Health is the largest non-life line: ₹1.17 lakh crore in premium in FY25 and about ₹1.37 lakh crore in FY26, covering about 58 crore lives. PM-JAY adds about 13 crore cumulative admissions across about 38,000–39,000 hospitals.

### Cited Findings
- **Health gross premium (general + SAHI, excl. PA/travel): FY24 ₹1,07,681 crore (+20.32%) and FY25 ₹1,17,505 crore (+9.12%).** FY25 mix by premium: group 52.3%, individual 39.7%, government 8.0% — [IRDAI AR 2024-25, Table I.26 (primary)](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- **Lives covered FY25: about 58 crore (5,806 lakh) under 2.65 crore policies.** Split: 42.3% government schemes, 47.4% group, 10.3% individual (individual = 601 lakh lives). FY24: about 57 crore lives — [IRDAI AR 2024-25 (primary)](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- Life insurers' health premium in FY25: ₹2,382 crore — [IRDAI AR 2024-25](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- **FY26 health premium: about ₹1.37 lakh crore (+15%)**. Top three: New India ₹21,532 crore, Star Health ₹18,435 crore, Care Health ₹9,768 crore — [Cafemutual (GI Council data)](https://cafemutual.com/news/insurance/37628-who-are-the-top-insurers-across-health-and-motor-business-in-fy26). CareEdge said retail health led FY26 non-life growth, which was 9.3% overall — [CareEdge Ratings](https://www.careratings.com/uploads/newsfiles/1776776989_Non%20Life%20Premiums%20Hit%20Rs%203.4%20Lakh%20Crore%20as%20Health%20Leads%20FY26%20Growth.pdf) (secondary; the FY26 figure may use a different scope from IRDAI's FY25 ₹1.175 lakh crore)
- Market structure at 31 Mar 2025: 27 general insurers plus 7 standalone health insurers, and 17 active TPAs — [IRDAI AR 2024-25](https://lifeinscouncil.org/component/IRDAI%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf)
- **PM-JAY (as of 31 Aug 2026): 13.25 crore hospital admissions worth ₹2.03 lakh crore**, through 38,000+ empanelled public and private hospitals (39,000+ by Sep 2026). 48.54 crore Ayushman cards. More than 60 crore people covered. 1,961 procedures — [Open Magazine, Sep 2026](https://openthemagazine.com/india/ayushman-bharat-at-8-60-crore-covered-203-lakh-crore-in-hospital-care) (secondary; NHA dashboard not checked directly)

### Inferences
- The FY25 claims "leakage" (₹29,933 crore disallowed plus repudiated) is about **25% of the year's health premium**. It is a larger pool than the whole premium of any single insurer (New India's FY26 health premium is ₹21,532 crore).
- Rough addressable activity: about 3.5 crore new private/commercial health claims a year, of which about 58% are cashless and about 69% are TPA-handled, flowing through about 2.7 lakh hospital–TPA network agreements.

### Gaps
- An authoritative count of unique network hospitals is not published. The 2.74 lakh TPA agreements include duplicates.
- PM-JAY claim rejection and deduction rates were not researched. These numbers are not from the NHA dashboard.
